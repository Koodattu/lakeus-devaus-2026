# Brief and principles

## The communication job

By the end of the session, participants should understand one central change:

> AI made plausible software abundant. The scarce parts of software development moved elsewhere.

They should leave with an evidence-grounded way to be optimistic without being naive. The aim is not to make the room agree about AI. It is to improve the quality of the disagreement.

## Audience

The core audience is the Lakeus-Devaus community: software developers and adjacent professionals in Seinäjoki and Etelä-Pohjanmaa. The event is public and free, so the talk must also work for students, managers, product people, entrepreneurs, and curious non-developers.

Assume a mixed room:

- active agent users and people who avoid AI entirely;
- consultants, product-company developers, students, and people between jobs;
- optimists, skeptics, and people who are simply tired of the subject;
- technical experts and visitors who should not need to understand model architecture.

## Format

- a complete 60-minute formal programme: 5-minute intro, 10-minute quick-fire gallup, 5-minute demo, 35-minute main presentation, and 5-minute recap;
- an optional discussion runway up to 90 minutes if the booking and audience energy allow it;
- no additional prepared lecture after the formal hour—extra time belongs to audience questions and selected appendix threads;
- presentation-led, not a workshop or step-by-step tutorial;
- conversational and willing to leave some questions open;
- live voting is part of the argument, not decoration; the opening questions stay deliberately short.

The audience promise: a four-year status report, a look at how development work is changing in practice, an honest examination of contradictory evidence, and an open question about where human judgment creates value next.

## Central thesis

### Short version

**Koodi halpeni. Vastuu ei.**

### Full version

AI has not made software engineering disappear. It has reduced the cost of producing a plausible first version and expanded who can participate. That moves the bottleneck toward:

- choosing a worthwhile problem;
- supplying context and constraints;
- evaluating whether the result is actually correct;
- security, maintenance, and operational ownership;
- integration with organizations and existing systems;
- product judgment, trust, attention, and distribution.

The human role therefore changes from being the sole producer of every line toward being the accountable editor, system designer, evaluator, and owner of consequences. Writing code still matters, but typing it is a smaller share of the value proposition.

## Important distinctions

The talk should repeatedly separate ideas that public discussion tends to collapse:

| Do not collapse | Better distinction |
| --- | --- |
| A convincing demo and a dependable system | Plausibility is cheap; reliability is earned over time. |
| Code generation and software engineering | Engineering includes deciding, integrating, securing, operating, and maintaining. |
| Individual speed and organizational delivery | Faster local output can still create review queues, instability, or coordination cost. |
| Open weights and open source | Model weights can be available while data, training code, and process remain closed. |
| AI correlation and AI causation | Consulting weakness also reflects macroeconomics, customer budgets, public spending, utilization, and post-pandemic adjustment. |
| A leaderboard score and useful capability | Benchmarks saturate, real tasks are jagged, and reliability matters. |
| More software and more value | Supply can grow faster than attention, trust, or willingness to pay. |

## Tone

Curious, concrete, locally grounded, and slightly provocative. Neither a vendor keynote nor a collapse sermon.

Use humor around AI slop and same-looking SaaS, but not contempt toward people who are experimenting, worried, junior, unemployed, or exhausted. The audience should feel invited to compare experience with evidence.

## Claims we can responsibly make

- AI use in software development is widespread, while trust in the output remains much lower than adoption.
- Depending on the task and environment, AI can accelerate work, slow it down, or move effort from creation into review and verification.
- Agent capability is improving quickly, but autonomy and reliability remain uneven.
- The marginal cost of a plausible prototype has fallen faster than the cost of ownership.
- AI amplifies the quality of the surrounding system: specifications, tests, architecture, review, feedback, and culture.
- Finnish IT consulting is under mixed pressure. AI is part of the change, but it is not a sufficient causal explanation for weak company results.
- As software supply expands, problem selection, trust, distribution, and durable ownership become more important.

## Claims to use only as questions or provocations

- “Software development changed more in 2026 than in 2000–2025.”
- “AI can do everything.”
- “Anyone can now build production software.”
- “AI caused the Finnish consulting downturn.”
- “SaaS is dead.”
- “Junior developers are disappearing.”

Each is useful as a headline to interrogate, not as an established fact.

## Non-goals

- a tutorial on prompting, agents, local models, quantization, or a specific tool;
- a comprehensive model leaderboard that will age before the event;
- investment advice or a prediction of specific company winners;
- legal advice about the EU AI Act;
- a complete history of AI;
- an attempt to settle whether AI is “good” or “bad”;
- a catalogue of every personal project or every available statistic.

## Success criteria

The session works if:

1. the opening and closing polls reveal something about the room, rather than merely collecting engagement;
2. participants can repeat the thesis in their own words;
3. the productivity evidence is presented as a real tension, not cherry-picked proof;
4. the local industry section distinguishes evidence from attribution;
5. at least one demonstration makes the new development loop tangible without becoming a tutorial;
6. the final discussion reaches responsibility, value, and human judgment—not only which model is best;
7. every factual slide has a date, source note, and honest caveat.
