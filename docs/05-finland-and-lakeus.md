# Finland and Lakeus-Devaus

## The responsible local claim

Finnish IT consulting is under real pressure, but the evidence does **not** support a one-line story that AI caused the downturn.

The more defensible interpretation is:

> A weak market, delayed customer decisions, constrained public and private budgets, utilization problems, post-pandemic adjustment, and company-specific execution are interacting with AI-driven expectations about price, speed, skills, and what clients should buy.

AI is simultaneously:

- a productivity tool;
- a reason for customers to reassess old delivery models;
- a new service category consultants hope to sell;
- an investment and reskilling cost;
- a source of uncertainty that can delay purchases;
- a convenient narrative that can be overused to explain ordinary business problems.

Call it **pressure and product**, not a proven single cause.

## Sector-level context

Technology Industries of Finland reported that Finnish IT services and software turnover grew approximately 9% in 2025 to more than €20 billion. In the first quarter of 2026:

- new orders were down 10% quarter over quarter but up 15% year over year;
- the order book was down 4% year over year;
- turnover was expected to remain roughly flat over the following six months;
- employment fell 0.6% quarter over quarter to approximately 79,200.

This is not an industry-wide collapse. It is a mixed market in which aggregate turnover can grow while employment, utilization, margins, or individual consulting businesses weaken.

Statistics Finland reported 9.7% unemployment in 2025, up 1.3 percentage points, and said employment decreased most in information and communication in the fourth quarter. The European Commission described Finland’s 2025 GDP growth as 0.2%, with weak labor-market conditions and fiscal consolidation in the background.

### Presentation meaning

Start with the macro and sector picture. Only then show company examples. Otherwise a handful of familiar firms can be mistaken for the entire Finnish software economy.

## Questions more useful than “Did AI cause this?”

1. If an agent reduces implementation effort, do clients buy the same project for fewer hours, buy more scope, or bring the work in-house?
2. What happens to hourly billing when clients expect AI productivity but delivery organizations still carry integration, security, and accountability?
3. Which consulting work depends on generic implementation, and which depends on domain access, change management, procurement, trust, and operating responsibility?
4. Does public-sector budget pressure delay digital work that still needs to happen later?
5. Can smaller regional firms compete through proximity and domain knowledge when raw code production is less differentiating?
6. How do consultancies train juniors if fewer routine delivery tasks are billable?

## A possible two-slide local sequence

### Slide A — “Suomen IT-markkina ei ole yksi käyrä”

Show three facts only:

- sector turnover grew in 2025;
- employment and order-book signals weakened;
- selected consultancies reported very different combinations of declining revenue, improving efficiency, restructuring, and AI investment.

Meaning: weak consulting performance is real, but the market is mixed.

### Slide B — “AI on paine ja tuote”

Use a two-sided visual:

| Pressure on the old model | Demand for a new model |
| --- | --- |
| Clients expect more output per euro | AI strategy and adoption work |
| Generic implementation is easier to internalize | Data, architecture, security, and integration |
| Seat/hour pricing comes under scrutiny | Accountable managed outcomes |
| Junior task mix changes | Reskilling and organizational redesign |

## Lakeus-Devaus continuity

Lakeus-Devaus describes itself as an active regional software community with more than 260 Slack members and the line “Mettäpöyröytymistä vastaan since 2016.” That gives the presentation permission to be locally specific and technically honest without becoming inward-looking.

The December 2024 event **“Paikalliset koodaavat kielimallit”** reportedly drew more than 50 participants. It covered local coding models, model characteristics, quantization, context, practical coding and debugging, and tools/models including Ollama and Qwen2.5-Coder.

### Editorial consequence

Do not repeat the 2024 workshop as a 2026 model catalogue. Use it as the opening of a local before/after:

> “Kaksi vuotta sitten kysyimme, saako paikallisen mallin auttamaan koodauksessa. Nyt kysymme, kuinka suuren lopputuloksen uskallamme antaa agentille—ja kuka tarkistaa, mitä sieltä tulee takaisin.”

The callback lets the audience feel the speed of change without a global-history lecture.

## Regional relevance without forced local color

The best local angle is not to claim that Seinäjoki follows different technical laws. It is to ask how global abundance changes the value of regional strengths:

- knowledge of local industries and operational realities;
- access to decision-makers and users;
- reputation that has to survive the next encounter;
- Finnish and European data, procurement, and regulatory context;
- willingness to own an unglamorous system after the prototype;
- community learning when individual companies cannot build every capability alone.

These strengths map directly to the thesis: context, trust, integration, and ownership become more valuable as generic production gets cheaper.

## Risks in this section

- Company reports are management narratives with incentives and should be attributed.
- The comparison periods differ. Label them on the slide and avoid precise cross-company ranking.
- A public discussion can include employees or former employees of named firms. Discuss reported business evidence, not personal performance.
- Do not imply that government cuts are the sole explanation without a quantified procurement series.
- Refresh all company and macro figures before the event; several firms may have published later reports by then.
